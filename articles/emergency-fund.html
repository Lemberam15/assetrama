Emergency fund: how much money is enough?

Every solid financial plan starts with one boring, unglamorous number — the one that lets you sleep when life goes wrong.

An emergency fund is money kept aside only for genuine emergencies — a job loss, a medical bill, an urgent repair. It is not an investment. Its job is not to grow; its job is to exist, instantly, on your worst day.

The rule of thumb: 6 months of expenses

Add up your truly essential monthly costs — rent or EMI, food, utilities, school fees, insurance premiums, transport. Multiply by 6. That's the standard target for a salaried person.

  • Job not so secure, or income varies? Aim for 9–12 months instead.
  • Dual-income household with stable jobs? 3–4 months may be reasonable.
Key fact: the fund is sized on essential expenses — what you must pay to keep life running — not your current full lifestyle spending.

Where to keep it

The whole point is that the money is safe and reachable within a day or two. Three common homes for it:

  • A separate savings account (not the one you spend from daily)
  • A fixed deposit that can be broken online
  • A liquid mutual fund — typically earns a little more, redemption lands in 1–2 days

What it should never be: stocks, equity mutual funds, gold, or any investment that can be down exactly when you need it. Emergencies and market crashes have a habit of arriving together.

The three mistakes that defeat the purpose

  1. Investing it for returns. Earning 2% less on idle money is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.
  2. Spending it on non-emergencies. A sale, a vacation, a new phone — none of these is an emergency. Once the line blurs, the fund disappears.
  3. Counting credit cards or loans as the fund. Borrowing during a crisis is the opposite of having a buffer — the fund exists precisely so you never have to.

How to build it without strain

You don't need it by next month. A common approach: first save 1 month of expenses as fast as possible (that alone covers most surprises), then automate a fixed amount every month until you reach 6 months. A bonus, a maturity, a refund — anything extra can top it up. If you ever use the fund, rebuild it first, before resuming other investments.

Quick recap

  1. Target 6 months of essential expenses (9–12 if income is unstable).
  2. Keep it in a savings account, FD or liquid fund — safe and reachable fast.
  3. Never invest it in equity; never spend it on non-emergencies.
  4. Build it in stages — 1 month first, then automate.
